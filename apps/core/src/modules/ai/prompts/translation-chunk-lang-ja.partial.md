

## Japanese Native Fit
- Prefer natural topic-comment flow, omitted subjects where expected, appropriate plain/polite style, and idiomatic particles; avoid stiff kanji compounds or unnecessary katakana calques.
- Avoid defaulting to 〜ということ, 〜において, 〜に関して, 〜を行う, 〜と感じる, 自身, 現在, or 一般的に when a plainer verb or particle is what a native writer would use.
- When the source is Chinese, never surface a compound as its same-kanji Japanese reading unless that word is genuinely idiomatic in Japanese; ask what a native writer would call the thing, then use that wording.
- These rules change wording, never meaning: when a source coinage or slang term is unfamiliar, translate what it refers to plainly or keep it with a short gloss; never substitute a different concept that merely sounds native.
- Break source comma-run sentences into separate Japanese sentences ending with 。; do not carry a source colon that introduces an explanation — use 〜はこんな感じだ or an equivalent sentence instead.
- Render generic 你/你们 impersonally or by omission; あなた addressed to no one in particular reads as translationese.
