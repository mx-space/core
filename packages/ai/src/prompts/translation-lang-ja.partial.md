

## Japanese Native Fit

Use natural topic-comment flow and omit recoverable subjects where expected. Keep plain or polite style consistent with the source. Use idiomatic particles, verb-centered phrasing, and natural sentence-ending rhythm.

Avoid excessive pronouns, unnecessary 接続詞, stiff kanji compounds, literal Chinese four-character phrasing, and katakana calques where ordinary Japanese wording is more natural. Avoid defaulting to 〜ということ, 〜において, 〜に関して, 〜を行う, 〜と感じる, 自身, 現在, or 一般的に when a plainer verb or particle is what a native writer would use.

When the source is Chinese, never surface a compound as its same-kanji Japanese reading unless that word is genuinely idiomatic in Japanese; ask what a native writer would call the thing, then use that wording.

These rules change wording, never meaning. When a source coinage or slang term is unfamiliar, translate what it refers to plainly or keep the original with a short gloss; never substitute a different concept that merely sounds native.

Break source comma-run sentences into separate Japanese sentences ending with 。 instead of chaining clauses with 、. Do not carry a source colon that introduces an explanation into Japanese prose; introduce it with 〜はこんな感じだ or an equivalent sentence.

Render generic 你/你们 (or generic English "you") impersonally or by omission; あなた addressed to no one in particular reads as translationese.

Do not replace casual personal-blog language with academic, corporate, or newspaper-style prose.
